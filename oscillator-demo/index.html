Releasing the mass at rest at displacement x₀ makes a coherent state |α⟩ with α = x₀/√2 — a superposition of many n, not a single eigenstate. Its number distribution is Poissonian, P(n) = e−|α|²|α|2n/n!. Free evolution would keep oscillating classically forever without collapsing; only an actual energy measurement forces a single random outcome n, with exactly these probabilities. That's why a small pull can still occasionally measure n=2 or n=3 — it doesn't deterministically round to the "nearest" level.

N atoms in a ring, each linked to its two neighbors by identical springs (stiffness K), with atom N linked back to atom 1 — periodic boundary conditions. In Fourier (normal-mode) coordinates the Hamiltonian decouples into N independent oscillators, one per allowed wavevector k: Ĥ = Σk [ (1/2m) p̂kp̂−k + ½ m ωk² x̂kx̂−k ],   ωk² = (4K/m) sin²(ka/2). Each of the N allowed k's is exactly the single-oscillator problem from the first tab, just with its own ωk — and its own independent occupation number nk. The full state is a product over every mode, |n0, n1, …, nN−1⟩ — âk / âk† raise or lower only the one mode k you're currently inspecting, leaving every other mode's occupation number untouched.

Same Hamiltonian as the first tab, plus one extra term: Ĥ = p̂²/2 + ½x̂² + λx̂⁴. Unlike the plain ½x̂² potential, this one has no closed-form eigenstates, so the energies En and wavefunctions ψn(x) shown here come from numerically diagonalizing Ĥ inside the truncated harmonic-oscillator basis |0⟩…|9⟩ — the same basis, Hermite functions, and turning-point logic as tab 1, just no longer an eigenbasis of this particular Ĥ. Two things follow directly: the levels stop being evenly spaced by ħω (the quartic wall stiffens faster than the parabola, so spacing grows with n instead of staying constant), and â / â† stop being ladder operators for these states — they still shift |k⟩ by one step in the harmonic basis, but that no longer maps one true eigenstate of Ĥ onto another, so they're not offered as controls here. Drag λ up from 0 to watch the parabola's evenly-spaced levels warp continuously into the quartic ones.

Hamiltonian in the harmonic-oscillator basis & eigenstate mixing

|⟨i|Ĥ|j⟩| heatmap

In the pure SHO, Ĥ is exactly diagonal in |0⟩…|9⟩ and every eigenstate is a single one-hot basis vector (see tab 1's matrix panel). Here λx̂⁴ only couples states of matching parity (|i−j| = 0, 2, or 4), so Ĥ has this banded structure instead — and diagonalizing it mixes several |k⟩ into each true eigenstate |n⟩, shown as the bar chart on the right instead of a single spike.

Second quantization: instead of a wavefunction ψ(x,y), the state lives in Fock space and particles are made by operators. Here a 4×4 lattice gives 16 single-particle modes. In position space a†r (bosons) or ĉ†r (fermions) puts a particle at site r=(x,y). In momentum space a†k = (1/4) Σr ei k·r a†r creates a plane wave with k=(2π mx/4, 2π my/4). Acting once on the vacuum gives a single particle either way; the statistics only show up when you create a second one — bosons stack (√(n+1) factors), fermions are Pauli-blocked and pick up sign flips (ĉ†rĉ†s = −ĉ†sĉ†r). Click a cell in either grid to pick the mode.
